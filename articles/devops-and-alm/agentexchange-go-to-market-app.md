# AgentExchange Go-To-Market App

The AgentExchange Go-To-Market (GTM) App is the tool ISV partners use to sell their managed packages through Salesforce as a marketplace transaction instead of reporting offline deals. A partner builds products and private offers in its Partner Business Org; once a customer accepts an offer, Salesforce creates the order, provisions licenses, and Stripe invoices the customer and pays the partner.[^s1][^s3]

## How it works

The app tracks each transaction milestone with five objects:[^s3]

- **Products**: what is sold, each tied to a Partner Product Catalog that the partner account manager sets up.[^s3]
- **Offers**: the pricing and contract terms the customer reviews; an offer is initial or add-on and references products.[^s3]
- **Orders**: created on acceptance with taxes and final amounts applied; visible only to the partner, they drive billing and provisioning.[^s3]
- **Invoices**: generated by Stripe and emailed to the customer as pending, then marked paid once the customer pays.[^s3]
- **Payouts**: Stripe bundles settled payments into a single daily payout to the partner, net of Salesforce fees.[^s3]

Setup starts with a request to the partner account manager or Support for onboarding. Admins then grant two permissions through a permission set: one to view and configure the app, and one to read and create offers. The partner connects a Stripe account from Setup, supplying business, tax (W-9) and payout bank details.[^s1]

Each product must be mapped to the licenses it grants, using either a Package License ID or a Permission Set ID, which is what makes provisioning automatic.[^s1][^s4] Traditional package licenses give every seat the same access from one pool, while permission set licenses, available only for second-generation packages, gate features with custom permissions and can be priced by tier.[^s4]

A partner creates an initial offer with the customer, contract term, service start date, validity date and recipient and billing emails, adds line items with quantity and a monthly unit price, then sends it. Sent offers can't be edited or withdrawn during the offer period. Add-on offers attach to an existing order, end with the original contract and are prorated from their start date.[^s1] Offer statuses run from Draft and Sent to Accepted, Rejected, Withdrawn, Error, Expired or Void, and orders move through New, Processing and Activated.[^s1]

On the customer side, users must opt in to partner offers on the Your Account page before they can receive them. Partners can also turn on a Request Private Offer button on a listing from the Partner Console, which creates a lead in the Partner Business Org.[^s6]

## Limits and gotchas

The prerequisites and considerations page sets tight limits, current as of Winter '27:[^s2]

- Partners must be enrolled ISVforce partners with a seat-based managed package, USD set up in the PBO, and updated PADA terms.[^s2]
- Only US customers who are new to that product are supported; layered and consumption-based pricing are not.[^s2]
- Contracts are 12 months, with NET30 terms, full upfront payment by ACH debit and an invoice one day before the start date.[^s2]
- Orders don't auto-renew, and cancellations and partner-initiated refunds aren't supported yet.[^s2]

"Net-new" is judged per product, not per partner. A customer who bought App A offline can buy App B through the GTM App, but more App A licenses must go through the original channel until that contract ends.[^s5]

## When to use it (and when not)

Net-new orders that meet the requirements must use the GTM App.[^s5] Existing Channel Order App contracts keep running; to move one over, the partner cancels the service order at least 7 days before renewal, then sends a GTM offer whose start date matches the cancelled order's expiration so licenses don't lapse.[^s5] Deals outside the limits above, such as non-US or consumption-priced deals, still need another channel.[^s2]

## Connections

The app is the partner-facing transaction layer of `kt:agentexchange` and runs inside the `kt:partner-business-org`.[^s1] It is an alternative to the `kt:channel-order-app`, the older offline route for reporting orders, and partners migrate COA customers to it at renewal.[^s5] Its license mapping overlaps with what the `kt:license-management-app` tracks, and tiered offers depend on `kt:permission-set-licenses` in `kt:second-generation-managed-packages`.[^s4] The Request Private Offer button is configured in the `kt:partner-console`, and marketplace pricing complements standard `kt:salesforce-pricing`.[^s6][^s3]

# NIST SP 800-171

NIST Special Publication 800-171, *Protecting Controlled Unclassified Information in Nonfederal Systems and Organizations*, sets out recommended security requirements for protecting the confidentiality of Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) when it sits outside federal systems. The requirements apply to the components of a nonfederal system that process, store or transmit CUI, and to the components that protect them.[^s1] The current edition, Revision 3, was published as final on 14 May 2024 and supersedes Revision 2 of January 2021.[^s1][^s2]

For Salesforce practitioners it matters because defense and federal contractors often want to run CUI-bearing work (contract data, technical data, case files) on a CRM platform. Their contracts make SP 800-171 the bar, and their choice of Salesforce environment has to fit it.[^s4][^s6]

## Scope and who it applies to

The publication is meant for federal agencies to use in contracts and other agreements with nonfederal organizations. It explicitly does not cover organizations operating a system on behalf of an agency; those fall under the federal requirements instead.[^s3] NARA is the Executive Agent of the CUI program, and the CUI Registry defines which categories of information count as CUI.[^s3]

The main contractual route in defense is DFARS clause 252.204-7012. It requires a contractor's covered systems to meet the version of SP 800-171 in effect when the solicitation was issued, unless the contracting officer authorizes otherwise. It also requires reporting cyber incidents within 72 hours of discovery.[^s4] CMMC Level 2 assessments are built on the same requirements, and Salesforce's own CMMC FAQ describes the framework as based on NIST 800-171 controls.[^s6]

## How the requirements are built

The requirements come from the SP 800-53 control catalog. NIST starts with the controls in the SP 800-53B moderate baseline and tailors out the ones that are primarily a federal responsibility, are not directly about CUI confidentiality, are already covered by other controls, or do not apply.[^s3] For that reason whole families are left out: PII processing and transparency (because PII is itself a CUI category), program management (no baseline), and contingency planning (which addresses availability, not confidentiality).[^s3]

Revision 3 groups the requirements into 17 families, numbered in a 03.xx.yy scheme (for example 03.01.01 Account Management). Alongside the familiar access control, audit, configuration management, identification and authentication, and system and communications protection, the list includes Planning, System and Services Acquisition, and Supply Chain Risk Management.[^s3][^s1] Compared with Revision 2, NIST restructured each requirement to align directly with SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls, introduced organization-defined parameters (ODPs) that agencies or organizations fill in, and applied new tailoring criteria. It also published a CUI overlay that shows the control-by-control tailoring.[^s2] The companion SP 800-171A Rev. 3 gives the assessment procedures, and SP 800-172 adds enhanced requirements for high-value CUI.[^s1][^s2]

## Cloud services and Salesforce

SP 800-171 lets organizations meet requirements directly or through external service providers.[^s3] DFARS 252.204-7012 adds a cloud-specific rule. If a contractor uses an external cloud service provider to store, process or transmit covered defense information, the provider must meet security requirements equivalent to the FedRAMP Moderate baseline, and must also comply with the clause's paragraphs on incident reporting and the preservation of images and packet captures for at least 90 days.[^s4]

Salesforce's Government Cloud Plus - Defense datasheet lists NIST SP 800-171 alongside IL5, FedRAMP High and IRS 1075 as requirements the offering helps customers meet. It describes a dedicated AWS GovCloud environment for defense organizations and approved contractors that can store and process CUI.[^s5] Salesforce's Government Cloud page says a third-party (3PAO) NIST 800-171 attestation was performed against Rev 2, not Rev 3. It also says the defense environment is limited to contractors directly supporting DoD mission owners with IL4 or IL5 data, who cannot buy it for internal use.[^s6]

Gotchas:

- **The version is set by the contract, not by NIST's latest publication.** DFARS points to the revision in effect at solicitation, so check which revision an attestation covers.[^s4][^s6]
- **Platform attestation is not customer compliance.** The customer still has to implement and document many requirements itself, such as account management, audit review and incident response, in its own org and processes.[^s3][^s5]
- **Eligibility is a separate question from controls.** Being able to meet 800-171 does not make a contractor eligible for Government Cloud Plus - Defense.[^s6]
